## The STAR Framework

### What is STAR?

- **S**ituation: Set the context (where, when, what was happening)
- **T**ask: Describe your responsibility or challenge
- **A**ction: Explain what YOU did (specific steps)
- **R**esult: Share the outcome (quantify if possible)

### STAR Timing Guide

- **Situation**: 15-20% (set the scene quickly)
- **Task**: 10-15% (your specific role)
- **Action**: 50-60% (the meat of your story)
- **Result**: 15-20% (impact and learnings)

Total: 90-120 seconds for most interview answers

### STAR Example

**Question**: "Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem."

**Weak Answer**: "Oh, we had this bug once and I fixed it. It was really hard but I figured it out eventually."

**STAR Answer**:

- **S**: "Last quarter, our payment system crashed during peak hours, affecting 10,000 users."
- **T**: "As the on-call engineer, I needed to restore service within our 30-minute SLA."
- **A**: "I first isolated the issue to our database connection pool. I scaled up capacity, implemented a temporary queue, and coordinated with the team to roll out a permanent fix."
- **R**: "Service was restored in 22 minutes. I then documented the incident and created monitoring to prevent recurrence. We haven't had that issue since."

## Validation

- Does each story have clear S, T, A, R components?
- Are your actions specific and first-person?
- Do your results include measurable outcomes?
- Is timing roughly 90-120 seconds?

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Understand STAR framework
- [ ] Have 5 stories ready to structure
- [ ] Can deliver STAR stories verbally
- [ ] Prepared for common interview questions

## Common Pitfalls

- **Vague situations**: Be specific about context
- **"We" instead of "I"**: Focus on YOUR actions
- **Missing results**: Always quantify impact
- **Too long**: Keep it under 2 minutes
- **No learning from failures**: Always include what you learned
