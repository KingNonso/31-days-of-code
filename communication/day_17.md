## The STATE Framework

### S - Share Your Facts

Start with objective observations, not conclusions.

- Not: "You don't respect my time."
- But: "The last three meetings started 20 minutes late."

### T - Tell Your Story

Share your interpretation cautiously.

- "I'm starting to wonder if these meetings aren't a priority..."

### A - Ask for Their Path

Invite their perspective genuinely.

- "But I could be wrong. What's your view?"
- "Help me understand what's happening."

### T - Talk Tentatively

Use language that invites dialogue, not defensiveness.

- "I might be missing something..."
- "From my perspective..."

### E - Encourage Testing

Make it safe to disagree.

- "Do you see it differently?"
- "I'd love to hear another perspective."

### Task 2: Practice Opening Lines (10 min)

The first 30 seconds set the tone. Practice these openers:

"I wanted to talk about something that's been on my mind. I value our relationship, and I want to understand your perspective..."

"This is awkward for me to bring up, but I think it's important enough that I'd rather have a difficult conversation than let it fester..."

"I've noticed [FACT]. I want to share my thoughts and hear yours..."

Record yourself delivering these openers.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Starting with conclusions**: Lead with facts
- **Blame language**: "You always..." triggers defense
- **Not listening**: Your goal is dialogue, not monologue
- **Avoiding the topic**: State the issue clearly
