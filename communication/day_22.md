# Day 22: Written Communication - Email Excellence

## Overview

**Topic**: Writing clear, effective emails and messages
**Estimated Time**: 60 minutes

- **Learn (15 min)**: Email best practices

## Email Best Practices

### The BRIEF Model

- **B**ackground: One sentence of context (if needed)
- **R**eason: Why you're writing
- **I**nformation: Key details
- **E**nd: Clear call to action
- **F**ollow-up: Next steps or deadline

### Subject Line Rules

- Specific: "Q3 Budget Approval Needed by Friday"
- Not: "Quick question" or "FYI" or "Hey"

### Email Structure

1. **First sentence**: What you need (BLUF)
2. **Middle**: Supporting details (bullets)
3. **Last sentence**: Clear next step with deadline

### Length Guidelines

- Ideal: Under 5 sentences
- Max: Scrollable on one screen
- If longer: Consider a meeting instead

### Task 3: Create Email Templates (10 min)

**Meeting Request Template:**
Subject: Meeting Request: [Topic] - [Duration needed]
Hi [Name],
[One sentence: why we need to meet]
Proposed times: [3 options]
[One sentence: what we'll accomplish]
Let me know what works.
[Your name]

**Create templates for:**

- Deadline reminder
- Status update
- Asking for feedback

### Task 4: Audit Your Sent Folder (10 min)

Review 5 recent emails you sent:

- Did you use BLUF?
- Was the subject line clear?
- Was there a clear call to action?
- Could it be shorter?

## Validation

- Can someone understand your email in 10 seconds?
- Is your call to action crystal clear?
- Would you want to receive this email?

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Understand BRIEF model
- [ ] Can write concise, clear emails
- [ ] Have reusable templates
- [ ] Audited real emails for improvement

## Common Pitfalls

- **Vague subject lines**: Be specific about content and action
- **Burying the ask**: BLUF—put request first
- **Wall of text**: Use bullets and white space
- **No deadline**: Always include when you need response
