## The LAER Framework

### L - Listen

- Don't interrupt
- Show you're hearing them
- Note their specific concern

### A - Acknowledge

- Validate their perspective
- Show empathy
- Don't dismiss or argue immediately

### E - Explore

- Ask clarifying questions
- Understand the root concern
- Find common ground

### R - Respond

- Address the specific concern
- Provide evidence or alternatives
- Propose a path forward

### Example in Action

**Objection**: "This will take too long and cost too much."

**L - Listen**: [Let them finish, nod, take notes]

**A - Acknowledge**: "I understand—timeline and budget are critical concerns, and you're right to flag them."

**E - Explore**: "Can you help me understand what timeline would work? And what budget constraints are we working with?"

**R - Respond**: "Given those constraints, here's how we could phase the project to deliver value faster while staying within budget..."

## Common Pitfalls

- **Arguing immediately**: Acknowledge first
- **Taking it personally**: It's about the idea, not you
- **Dismissing concerns**: Every objection has validity
- **Over-explaining**: Address the concern, don't lecture
