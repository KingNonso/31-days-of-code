## Slide Design Principles

### The 6x6 Rule

- Maximum 6 bullet points per slide
- Maximum 6 words per bullet
- If you need more, split into multiple slides

### Visual Hierarchy

1. **Title**: What's this slide about?
2. **Key Point**: One main idea
3. **Support**: Visual or brief text

### Less is More

- **Bad**: Dense paragraphs
- **Good**: Key phrases + you explain
- **Best**: Image/diagram + you explain

### Font and Color

- **Font size**: Minimum 24pt for readability
- **Contrast**: Dark text on light OR light text on dark
- **Colors**: 2-3 max, consistent throughout
- **Fonts**: 1-2 max, professional and readable

### Image Guidelines

- High quality (no pixelation)
- Relevant to content
- Minimal text overlay
- Consistent style

## Tasks

### Task 1: Audit Bad Slides (10 min)

Find examples of bad slides (online or your own). Identify:

- What makes them ineffective?
- How would you fix them?

Common issues:

- [ ] Too much text
- [ ] Inconsistent formatting
- [ ] Poor color contrast
- [ ] Irrelevant images
- [ ] Cluttered layout

### Task 2: Create Slides for Day 18 Presentation (20 min)

Create 5-7 slides:

**Slide 1 - Title**:

- Presentation title
- Your name
- Visual element

**Slide 2 - Hook/Agenda**:

- Attention-grabber
- Three points preview

**Slides 3-5 - Main Points**:

- One point per slide
- Supporting visual or key stat
- Minimal text

**Slide 6 - Summary**:

- Three points recap
- Visual representation

**Slide 7 - Call to Action**:

- Clear next step
- Contact/resource info

## Validation

- Can someone understand your slides without you?
- Do slides enhance your talk, not replace it?
- Is text readable from a distance?

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Understand slide design principles
- [ ] Created clean, minimal slides
- [ ] Practiced presenting WITH slides
- [ ] Can identify and fix bad slides

## Common Pitfalls

- **Wall of text**: If they're reading, they're not listening
- **Fancy animations**: Distract from content
- **Inconsistent design**: Looks unprofessional
- **Reading slides aloud**: Never read what they can see
