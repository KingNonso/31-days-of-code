## Presentation Structure

### The Classic Three-Part Structure

1. **Opening** (10%): Hook + agenda
2. **Body** (80%): 3 main points with support
3. **Closing** (10%): Summary + call to action

### Opening Elements

- **Hook**: Question, statistic, story, or bold statement
- **Credibility**: Brief, relevant qualification
- **Preview**: "I'll cover three things today..."

### Body Organization (Rule of Three)

- **Point 1**: Evidence + example
- **Point 2**: Evidence + example
- **Point 3**: Evidence + example
- Transitions between each

### Closing Elements

- **Summary**: "To recap, we covered..."
- **Call to Action**: What should they do?
- **Memorable close**: Circle back to hook or inspiring statement

### Task 2: Practice Delivery (15 min)

Record your presentation. Focus on:

**Voice**:

- [ ] Varied pace (not monotone)
- [ ] Strategic pauses
- [ ] Emphasis on key words

**Body Language** (if video):

- [ ] Eye contact (look at camera/audience)
- [ ] Open posture
- [ ] Purposeful gestures

**Content**:

- [ ] Clear structure
- [ ] Smooth transitions

## Validation

- Does your opening grab attention?
- Are your three points clear and supported?
- Did you minimize filler words?
- Is your call to action specific?

## Success Criteria

- [ ] Understand presentation structure
- [ ] Created complete presentation outline
- [ ] Practiced and recorded delivery
- [ ] Identified and improved weakness

## Common Pitfalls

- **Weak opening**: Don't start with "Um, so, today I'm going to..."
- **No clear structure**: Audience should know where you are
- **Reading slides**: Slides support you, not replace you
- **Trailing off**: End strong, not with "So, yeah..."
